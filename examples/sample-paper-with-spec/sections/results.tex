\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:results}

% @p p-9
We replay a 24-hour trace of 1{,}200 functions sampled from a public production
dataset~\cite{shahrad2020serverless} on a 32-core host. We compare
\textsc{Prefetch} with on-demand snapshot restore and with a warm pool whose
keep-alive window is tuned to match \textsc{Prefetch}'s 99th-percentile latency.

% @p p-10
Table~\ref{tab:latency} reports cold-start latency. \textsc{Prefetch} reduces
median latency from 412\,ms to 133\,ms relative to on-demand restore, because
the working set (Equation~\ref{eq:working-set}) covers most of the pages each
invocation touches. 

\begin{table}[t]
  \centering
  \caption{Cold-start latency in milliseconds.}
  \label{tab:latency}
  \begin{tabular}{lrrr}
    \toprule
    System & p50 & p99 & Memory (GB) \\
    \midrule
    On-demand restore & 412 & 1630 & 18 \\
    Warm pool         &  21 &  480 & 96 \\
    \textsc{Prefetch} & 133 &  478 & 36 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

% @p p-11
The warm pool achieves lower median latency, since most of its requests hit a
warm instance, but it holds 96\,GB of memory against 36\,GB for
\textsc{Prefetch} at the same tail latency.
