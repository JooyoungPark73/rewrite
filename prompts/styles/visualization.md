## The field's question

Does the representation support the analytical task it is meant for, for the people who will
use it? A system can run perfectly and still fail as a tool for thinking. Visualization sits
between graphics, HCI and perception, so its evidence borrows from each.

## What counts as evidence

Human task performance (accuracy, time, error) on the intended task; comparison with the
representations people would otherwise use; perceptual grounding for encoding choices;
case studies or deployments with domain experts, described well enough to judge; for
techniques, scalability in data size and visual clutter. The boundary of a claim is the task,
the data characteristics, the user population and the display.

## What performance and contribution mean

Performance is how well people complete the analytical task with the representation, not
frame rate alone (though rendering limits count). A contribution is a technique, system or
evaluation that changes what people can see or reason about, shown on the task it claims.

## What a reviewer here looks for first

- a technique justified by aesthetics or novelty with no task;
- a user study whose task is not the one the paper claims to support;
- an encoding that contradicts perceptual findings without acknowledging it;
- scalability claimed from a small or cherry-picked dataset;
- "insight" or "understanding" reported without saying what was found or by whom;
- a system-only evaluation (it works) where a human evaluation is the claim.

## Vocabulary

Fits: supports task T, participants could, compared with, accuracy and time, scales to N
items, encodes X as Y. Misleads: intuitive, insightful, beautiful, effective (without the
task), interactive (without what the interaction achieves), explores (without a finding).
