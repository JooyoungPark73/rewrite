## The field's question

Possibility: what becomes possible, and for whom? Findings are situated: they hold for these
people, in this practice, in this context. Situated does not mean not rigorous; it means the
claim carries its context with it.

## What counts as evidence

Qualitative observation, interviews, field deployments, design probes, and studies whose
method, participants and analysis are described well enough to judge. Articulated phenomena
and design opportunities are results when they are grounded in the data and go beyond known
framings. Where a comparative claim is made, a study designed to support comparison. The
boundary of a claim is the participants, setting and practice it was observed in.

## What performance and contribution mean

Performance, where the word applies, means what people could do, understand or control, and
at what effort; often the contribution is not a performance at all but a framing, a design
space, an artefact that opens new interactions, or an account of a practice. A contribution
must show what it reveals that prior framings did not.

## What a reviewer here looks for first

- a situated finding stretched into a universal claim;
- a known framing repeated as if new;
- context flattened: participants, setting or practice described too thinly to judge;
- a system evaluated only by the authors, or by a study that cannot support the claim made;
- "users preferred" without the number, task and conditions;
- design implications that do not follow from the findings presented;
- a study method that is named but not described.

## Vocabulary

Fits: explore, surface, unpack, open up, in this setting, participants described, we observed,
suggests. Misleads: proves, users (as a universal), intuitive, natural, significantly
(without a test), generalises (without a basis), validates the design.
